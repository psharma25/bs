Position

Read this before the detail.

Secureframe presents an unusually strong published compliance posture for a company of its size — SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, CMMC Level 2 certification, TX-RAMP, and a FedRAMP 20x Low authorization, with the FedRAMP POA&M and control test results published for public download. That last point is materially more transparent than the market norm and should be credited.

The concerns are not about control design. They are about currency of evidence and concentration of access. The publicly advertised SOC 2 report period ended more than a year before this assessment date, and the FedRAMP authorization letter is within days of its stated validity end. Separately, a compliance automation platform necessarily holds broad authenticated read access into the identity provider, cloud accounts, code repositories, and HR system of every customer — a wider view of the environment than almost any other vendor in a typical stack. That access profile, not the control maturity, is what drives the Tier 1 recommendation.

Two items to resolve before contract. The current SOC 2 Type II report covering the period after 30 June 2025, or a bridge letter; and written confirmation of the FedRAMP 20x authorization status past its stated letter expiry. Neither is likely to be a problem — both are almost certainly a publication lag rather than a lapse — but both must be evidenced rather than assumed.

What this assessment could not cover

Stated plainly so the gaps are not mistaken for clean results.

  • No active technical observation. TLS configuration, certificate chain, HSTS, security headers, SPF/DKIM/DMARC records, certificate transparency logs, and subdomain enumeration were all out of reach from the assessment environment. Each is a ten-minute check from an unrestricted workstation and should be completed before sign-off.
  • No gated documents. The SOC 2 report, ISO certificate, pen test summary, CMMC certificate, and shared responsibility matrix all sit behind a request-and-NDA workflow. Their existence is confirmed; their contents are not.
  • No OAuth scope review. The single most informative available check — what permissions the Secureframe application actually requests in your identity provider — requires access to your own tenant, not theirs.
  • No contract or DPA review. Notification SLAs, liability, and subprocessor change terms are unassessed.

Why not A

Three items unverified rather than verified-bad, plus one structural ceiling.

  • SOC 2 is stale on its face. The advertised period ended thirteen months before assessment date. The independent attestation is the strongest single input to an A grade and it cannot be confirmed as current. Almost certainly a publication lag — but "almost certainly" is not an A.
  • No published terms on the two AI subprocessors. OpenAI and Twilio sit in the chain with no stated data location, while the platform ingests the customer's policy library, control evidence and personnel records. Training use, retention and residency are all blank.
  • No encryption-at-rest control in the public catalogue. In-transit is published; at-rest is not. It almost certainly exists and is simply unpublished, but an assessor cannot grade what is not visible.
  • Structural ceiling. A compliance automation platform holds broader authenticated read access into the customer estate than almost anything else purchased. The Vanta precedent (Section 7) shows that risk materialising in this exact category, via a code change rather than an attacker. This caps the achievable grade for every vendor in the class, not just this one.

Why not C — and not close

The control programme is demonstrably strong and better evidenced than most peers at this size.

  • SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, CMMC Level 2 via Cyber AB with C3PAO Redspin valid to September 2028, FedRAMP 20x Low, and TX-RAMP Level 1.
  • A dedicated CISO with over four years in seat who took a prior company through FedRAMP authorization. Security ownership is unambiguous and not a part-time CTO responsibility.
  • They publish their own POA&M. The open findings list, the 3PAO assessment methodology, and machine-readable control test results are all open download with no NDA. This is the opposite of a company concealing its weak points, and it is rare enough to be worth weighting.
  • No breach disclosure, no regulatory enforcement, no attributed CVE.
  • A published control catalogue with named, testable controls across access, change, availability, incident response and risk — not marketing language.

Recommendation

Proceed to contract, conditional on the four A-grade items closing before signature rather than after. Conditions to carry into the agreement:

  • Tier 1 treatment with annual reassessment
  • Named business owner on the risk acceptance — not security
  • Incident notification SLA written into the security exhibit, measured from discovery, rather than left to standard terms
  • Subprocessor change notification with a defined objection window
  • Attestation expiry tracked as a monitored date, given that the current lapse is what produced finding F-01
Framing note for client presentation. This is a B on the evidence available to a passive assessment, not a B on the company. Most of what holds it off an A is one document request away. If the residual questions in Section 10 return fast and specific answers, expect to re-grade upward — and say so when presenting, so the grade is not mistaken for a verdict on the vendor's competence.

Vulnerability databases — and why the null result means little

No CVE is attributed to Secureframe in the National Vulnerability Database, and no vendor security advisory was found. Confirmed

Do not read this as a clean result. CVE identifiers attach to distributable software and hardware, not to hosted services. A pure multi-tenant SaaS with no on-premises agent or downloadable component will accumulate essentially no CVEs regardless of how many vulnerabilities it has actually had, because there is no versioned artefact for a CVE to describe. The same is true of every competitor in this category. The absence of CVEs carries close to zero signal and must not be presented to a client as evidence of a strong posture.

The meaningful proxy in this category is the vendor's own published FedRAMP POA&M, which is available for open download and lists actual open findings. That document should be read directly before any award decision.

Why this matters more than any finding about Secureframe specifically. The exposed material was not customer personal data. It was the security configuration of the customer's own environment — precisely the artefact a compliance automation platform exists to collect. A party holding another organisation's MFA configuration and security tooling inventory holds a targeting map. This is the concentration risk in Section 1 and finding F-03 moving from theory to documented event.
  • The realistic threat model for this vendor class is a tenant-isolation defect, not a breach. No attacker was involved. Assessment effort should therefore weight multi-tenancy architecture and isolation testing far above perimeter controls, which is the opposite of how most questionnaires are weighted.
  • The response is the benchmark. Nine days to remediation, individual notification, named executive accountability. Any vendor in this category should be held to that standard, and it is a fair thing to say out loud in a diligence call.
  • No equivalent event is known for Secureframe. That is a genuine distinction, but the base rate for the category is now non-zero and the architecture is comparable.

Product capability limitation — vulnerability management module

Not a security weakness in the vendor. A material limitation if the platform is intended to serve as the vulnerability management control of record.

  • Vulnerability data is sourced only from the customer's connected scanners — AWS Inspector, Tenable, Qualys, Wiz, Dependabot and similar. The platform does not independently query NVD, CVE.org, OSV, or the GitHub Advisory Database. Coverage is therefore only as good as the scanners connected, with no independent cross-check. Confirmed from vendor documentation.
  • False positives cannot be closed or dismissed within the platform; they must be addressed in the source system. A second version of the module is described as in development. Confirmed
  • Severity uses standard CVSS tiers only; alternative classification frameworks are not supported. Confirmed
Consequence for the engagement: if a client intends to rely on this module to evidence a vulnerability management control, the evidence chain runs through the connected scanners, not through the platform. Design the control and its evidence accordingly, and do not describe the platform as the scanning capability in an SSP or system description.

Availability history — independent monitors

Two independent monitoring services have tracked the vendor's public status page since 2022 and 2023 respectively. Between them they record on the order of 52 to 70 incidents, averaging roughly 1.4 per month, with a median resolution time near five hours. Acknowledged outages continue into 2026. Confirmed

Assessed against no published RTO or RPO. This is the reality check on any availability assertion in a questionnaire response, and it is independently sourced rather than vendor-supplied, which places it higher on the reliability hierarchy than most of this report.

Reconnaissance still outstanding

Each is fast and free. Complete before sign-off.

  • Certificate transparency logs (crt.sh) — highest yield of the outstanding checks. Reveals every subdomain a certificate has been issued for, exposing forgotten staging environments, internal tooling and acquisitions. No account required.
  • TLS and header inspection — cipher suites, protocol versions, HSTS, CSP, cookie flags.
  • Email authentication — SPF, DKIM and DMARC records. A permissive or absent DMARC policy on a security vendor is informative.
  • Censys or Shodan — internet-facing asset inventory. Expect a thin result for a CDN-fronted single-cloud SaaS; the value is in spotting forgotten assets rather than in the main estate.
  • Commercial ratings — useful only as change detection on a Tier 1 vendor. These infer from outside-in signal and misattribute assets routinely. A rating must never be presented to a client as an assessment.
  • OAuth scope review in the client's own identity provider — the single most informative check available, and it requires the client's tenant rather than the vendor's cooperation.

Check catalogue

Every check, its point value, and the criterion applied. Disclosed so a vendor can dispute a score on its merits — which a commercial rating does not permit.

CategoryCheckPointsFull-credit criterionPartial credit

Scan methodology

Collection. DNS record lookups (TXT, CAA, NS, DNSKEY, and DKIM selector probes against fourteen common selector names); a single TLS handshake on port 443 recording protocol, cipher, chain and certificate validity; a single HTTPS GET of the root path recording response headers and cookie flags; requests to /.well-known/security.txt and /security.txt; and a query to public certificate transparency logs for issued hostnames.

Explicitly not performed. Port scanning, service enumeration, vulnerability probing, credential testing, or any request to an endpoint the target has not published. Everything collected is what a browser and dig already do. No authorisation from the target is required and the tool should not be extended into active testing without written permission.

Scoring rules. Each check awards points against a possible value. Category score is the percentage earned within that category; the total is the weighted sum. Grade bands are A at 90 and above, B at 80, C at 70, D at 60, F below.

  • Unknown results award half credit. A collection failure should neither reward nor penalise the target. Unknowns are counted and displayed separately so a degraded scan cannot masquerade as a clean one.
  • Negative results are scored as observed, not as inferred. A missing DKIM selector is scored at partial credit with an explicit note that selectors are arbitrary strings and absence proves nothing.
  • TLS interception is detected and flagged. If the certificate chain is issued by an entity not on the recognised public CA list, the scan writes an error stating that all transport findings describe the intercepting middlebox rather than the target. This failure mode is real: a scan run from a corporate network with TLS inspection will otherwise score the proxy and file the result as vendor evidence.
  • Certificate transparency is treated as surface inventory, not vulnerability. A large footprint or the presence of non-production hostnames reduces score because it indicates inventory and decommissioning weakness, not because those hosts are known to be exploitable.
Interpretation limit — state this whenever the grade is shown. This measures externally observable hygiene only. It says nothing about internal controls, data handling, tenant isolation, or incident response — which, per the sector precedent in Section 7, is where the actual risk in this vendor category lives. A grade of A here is entirely compatible with poor internal security. Commercial ratings services measure approximately this same surface plus breach feeds and IP reputation; treat any such score, including this one, as change detection rather than assessment.

    Section 12

    Method and evidence standard

    Sources used

    • Vendor trust center, read directly — controls catalogue, compliance badges, resource library, subprocessor register
    • Vendor newsroom and blog — leadership announcements
    • Corporate aggregators — PitchBook, Tracxn, Crunchbase, Wellfound, and press profiles for funding, headcount and location
    • Third-party availability monitors — independent outage and incident history
    • Public search for breach, enforcement and vulnerability disclosure

    Grading methodology

    How Section 2 was produced, so the grade can be defended or disputed on its reasoning rather than accepted on authority.

    Scale. Three points. A — secure; can safeguard our data; proceed. B — some risks; proceed with conditions attached to contract. C — bad; do not proceed on current evidence. Plus and minus modifiers indicate position within a band and carry no independent meaning.

    Inputs, in descending weight.

    InputWeightRationale
    Currency and coverage of independent attestationHighestAn in-period third-party opinion is the only input not supplied by the vendor about itself. Staleness is weighted as heavily as absence, because an expired attestation and no attestation are the same evidentiary position.
    Blast radius of the relationshipHighWhat compromise of this vendor would yield. Structural, not remediable by controls, and therefore caps the achievable grade rather than reducing it.
    Unresolved data-handling questionsHighTraining use, retention, residency and isolation. Weighted higher than a known-bad answer would be weighted, because unknown risk cannot be priced or accepted.
    Published control programmeMediumNamed, testable controls score; marketing language does not. Vendor-published and therefore the weakest reliability class.
    Voluntary transparencyMediumPublishing an open findings list is costly to the vendor and therefore credible in a way that a certification badge is not. Weighted as a positive signal about culture, with the limits of that inference acknowledged.
    Incident and enforcement historyMediumSector base rate is considered alongside vendor-specific history, since a null result for one vendor in a young category means little on its own.
    Availability recordLowIndependently sourced and therefore reliable, but availability is rarely the deciding factor for a Tier 1 compliance dependency.
    Corporate and financial signalLowContinuity consideration only. Not weighted heavily on aggregator data that disagrees with itself.

    Rules applied. An unverified item is scored as unverified, never as satisfied and never as failed — the grade reflects what is known, and the residual questions exist to convert unknowns. A single sufficient condition can drop a grade without needing corroboration, because one disqualifying answer on data handling is disqualifying regardless of what else is strong. Structural risk inherent to the product category caps the ceiling and is applied equally to every vendor in that category, so a comparison between two compliance platforms is unaffected by it.

    What the grade is not. It is not a security rating in the commercial sense — no outside-in scanning contributed to it. It is not a statement about the vendor's competence. It is not transferable to a different use case: the same vendor assessed for a relationship with narrower access would grade differently, because blast radius is an input. And it is not durable — it should be re-issued on any of the reassessment triggers, and in any case at the next annual review.

    Evidence standard applied

    The same six properties applied to any control evidence, regardless of regime.

    • Traceable to source — every claim above can be traced to a named source class
    • Complete for the population — not met for headcount, location or revenue, hence the disputed markings
    • Covers the period — not met for the SOC 2, hence finding F-01
    • Contemporaneous — trust center read on the assessment date; aggregator data lags by an unknown interval
    • Integrity-protected — not applicable to passive collection
    • Attributable — vendor-published material is attributable to the vendor and is therefore the weakest reliability class where it is self-serving; independent monitors rank higher
    Reliability caution. The bulk of the positive findings in this report derive from the vendor's own published material. Under the standard reliability hierarchy that is the weakest evidence class available, below independently obtained and system-generated evidence. It is appropriate for a pre-engagement screen; it is not sufficient for contract award without the gated documents and the technical checks noted in Section 1.
