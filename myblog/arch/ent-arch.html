What enterprise architecture actually is

Not a diagram library. It is the discipline of making sure that what the business is trying to do, what it knows, what it runs, and what it runs on all point in the same direction — and that a decision made in one place does not quietly break another.

The job, in one sentence

You maintain a defensible chain from business capability to data domain to application to infrastructure, so that when someone proposes something, you can say what it costs, what it breaks and what it makes possible.

If you cannot trace a technology decision upward to a business capability, you are doing solution architecture and calling it enterprise architecture.

The one that saves you. Decision records. In two years someone will ask why the claim mart is in Redshift and not Athena, and the only defensible answer is a dated record naming the options, the constraint and the person who accepted the trade-off. Without it every past decision is relitigated from scratch.

Four questions to ask before any design

Decision
  • What decision does this data support?
  • Who makes it, and how often?
  • What happens if it is a day late?
Latency
  • Seconds, minutes, hours or days?
  • Is that a real need or a preference?
  • What does the source system actually emit?
Sensitivity
  • PHI, PII, de-identified or public?
  • Minimum necessary for whom?
  • Which contracts constrain it?
Ownership
  • Which domain owns the golden record?
  • Who approves access?
  • Who is paged when it breaks?

DOCArchitecture decision record — the template that saves you

One page, appended never rewritten. In two years this is the only defence you have against relitigating a settled choice.

docs/adr/ADR-014-iceberg-silver.md
# ADR-014: Iceberg for the silver zone

Status:      Accepted
Date:        2026-03-11
Deciders:    P. Sharma (EA), J. Okafor (Data Eng), M. Reyes (Claims steward)
Supersedes:  ADR-009 (Parquet with daily full rewrite)

## Context
Claims arrive as resubmissions and corrections. CDC from the core admin
platform emits updates and deletes. A daily full rewrite of the claim
partition costs 4.2 hours and blocks the 06:00 actuarial refresh.

## Options considered
1. Parquet + full partition rewrite   — simple, too slow, no row-level delete
2. Apache Hudi                        — good merge story, smaller AWS support surface
3. Apache Iceberg                     — merge-on-read, native Glue/Athena/EMR support
4. Redshift as system of record       — cost scales with retention, not with query

## Decision
Iceberg, format-version 2, partitioned by service_month.

## Consequences
+ MERGE on natural key handles resubmission and CDC with one code path
+ Time travel gives us the "what did we submit to CMS in March" answer for free
- Requires Glue 4.0+ and adds compaction as an operational job we must run
- Team needs Iceberg maintenance training before go-live

## Constraint that decided it
CMS encounter resubmission requires proving what we held at submission time.
Time travel gives that. Nothing else on the list did.
DOCArchitecture principles — the eight that settle arguments

Few, sharp, and each one rules something out. A principle that permits every option is a slogan.

docs/principles.md
1.  The system of record is never the analytics source.
    Implication: CDC out of core admin. No analytical query against production.

2.  Raw arrives untouched and is never edited.
    Implication: bronze is immutable. Defects are reprocessed forward.

3.  Every dataset has one named steward.
    Implication: no dataset ships without an owner in the catalogue.

4.  Classification decides handling, not the project.
    Implication: PHI controls are inherited from the tag, not negotiated.

5.  Minimum necessary applies internally.
    Implication: internal fan-out is filtered like external sharing.

6.  Latency is justified by a decision, not by preference.
    Implication: streaming requires a named decision that expires.

7.  Access is granted on tags, never on table names.
    Implication: the taxonomy is designed before the second table.

8.  If it is not in the catalogue, it does not exist.
    Implication: an undocumented pipeline is a defect, not a shortcut.

CHOOSING THE LANE

Streaming, batch and mixed

Choose streaming when

  • The decision expires. A care-gap alert after discharge is worthless.
  • The source emits events anyway — ADT feeds, portal activity.
  • You can tolerate out-of-order arrival and design for it.

Cost is per event and never sleeps. Ten thousand events an hour is cheap; ten million is a budget conversation.

Choose batch when

  • The source is a file. An 837 drop is a batch whatever you wrap around it.
  • The decision is periodic — monthly submissions, annual measures.
  • You need the whole window to compute correctly.

Cheapest per record and easiest to reason about. Most of a payer's value lives here and it is unfashionable to say so.

Choose mixed when

  • You need current state from a system you must not query directly.
  • Updates matter as much as inserts — a claim changes status repeatedly.
  • The consumer wants a table, not a stream.

Hardest to operate. Upserts, late arrivals and schema drift are the entire job. Iceberg or Hudi exists for exactly this.

A rule that will save you a year. Latency requirements are stated in meetings and discovered in production. Ask what the consumer does in the gap. If a nightly refresh means someone waits until morning to make a call they could make at nine anyway, you do not have a streaming requirement — you have a habit.

SQLIceberg table and the MERGE that handles corrections and CDC

One code path for resubmissions, corrections and change data capture. This is the reason Iceberg is in the design rather than plain Parquet.

sql/silver_claim_line.sql
-- Silver claim line, created once
CREATE TABLE glue_catalog.bcbs_lakehouse.claim_line (
    claim_control_number    string,
    line_number             int,
    enterprise_member_id    string,
    billing_provider_npi    string,
    service_date            date,
    service_month           string,
    procedure_code          string,
    diagnosis_codes         array<string>,
    billed_amount           decimal(12,2),
    allowed_amount          decimal(12,2),
    claim_status            string,
    source_file             string,
    ingested_at             timestamp,
    is_deleted              boolean
)
USING iceberg
PARTITIONED BY (service_month)
TBLPROPERTIES (
    'format-version' = '2',
    'write.delete.mode' = 'merge-on-read',
    'write.update.mode' = 'merge-on-read'
);

-- Applied on every run: resubmission, correction and CDC all land here
MERGE INTO glue_catalog.bcbs_lakehouse.claim_line AS t
USING staged_claims AS s
   ON t.claim_control_number = s.claim_control_number
  AND t.line_number = s.line_number

WHEN MATCHED AND s.op = 'D' THEN
    UPDATE SET is_deleted = true, ingested_at = s.ingested_at

WHEN MATCHED AND s.ingested_at > t.ingested_at THEN
    UPDATE SET *                       -- last writer on the natural key wins

WHEN NOT MATCHED THEN
    INSERT *;

-- What we submitted to CMS in March, months later, for free:
SELECT count(*), sum(billed_amount)
FROM   glue_catalog.bcbs_lakehouse.claim_line
FOR    TIMESTAMP AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-04-01 00:00:00'
WHERE  service_month = '2026-03';

ETL, ELT, and where each one belongs

The letters describe where transformation happens relative to landing. That single choice drives cost, auditability and how fast you can answer a question you have not been asked yet.

ETL — transform, then land

Data is reshaped in flight and only the result is stored. The classic warehouse pattern, and still correct in specific places.

Use it when:

  • You must not persist the raw form. Tokenising a member identifier or stripping a direct identifier before it reaches storage is an ETL decision.
  • A contract forbids retention of the source payload.
  • The transformation reduces volume enormously and the raw form has no future use.
  • The target is a fixed schema you control and query patterns are known.

Cost: you cannot answer a question you did not anticipate, because the raw data is gone.

ELT — land, then transform

Raw arrives in bronze untouched; transformation happens with elastic compute reading from storage. This is the default for a modern lakehouse.

Use it when:

  • Storage is cheaper than re-acquiring the data — which for claims and clinical feeds it always is.
  • Future questions are unknown, which is every analytics programme honestly described.
  • You need to reprocess history when a rule changes, and rules change constantly in risk adjustment and quality.
  • You want lineage that starts at the byte you received.

Cost: you are now storing PHI in its rawest form, and every control in the governance overlay has to be real.

Situation in this estatePatternWhy
837 claim files from trading partnersELTLand the X12 verbatim in bronze. You will reprocess it when an edit rule changes, and you will need to prove what the partner actually sent.
HL7v2 ADT event streamELT with an ETL stepLand raw, but redact free-text notes in flight before they reach any zone an analyst can reach.
Data shared with a research partnerETLDe-identification happens before it lands anywhere outside the boundary. The raw form must never exist on the far side.
CDC from the core admin platformELTLand the change events, merge into an Iceberg table. Merge logic changes far more often than the events do.
Pharmacy accumulator files from the PBMELTYou do not control the format and it will change without notice. Keep the original.
Price transparency machine-readable filesETL on the way outGold to a published artefact in a mandated schema. This is a write-side transformation, not a landing one.

The rule for a covered entity

Default to ELT so you can reprocess and prove lineage. Reach for ETL only where a legal or contractual obligation says a form of the data must not exist at rest — then do that transformation as early in the path as the obligation demands, and document which obligation drove it.

Never ETL because someone thinks storage is expensive. Storage is the cheapest thing in this architecture.

Trap. ELT is used as an excuse to skip modelling. Landing everything in bronze feels like progress and produces nothing. Silver is where the actual work is, and it is the layer teams underestimate by a factor of three.

CODEThe ETL case — tokenising before anything lands

The one situation where transformation must happen in flight: the raw form is not permitted to exist at rest on the far side of a boundary. Everything else in this platform is ELT.

jobs/research_extract_deidentify.py
"""Research extract: de-identify in flight, land only the result.

Driven by the data use agreement with the research partner, not by
preference. The raw form must never exist in the partner boundary, so
this is genuinely ETL and the ADR records which clause required it.
"""
import hashlib
import hmac
import os
from datetime import date

import boto3

# Key lives in Secrets Manager, rotated annually, never in the job.
_HMAC_KEY = boto3.client("secretsmanager").get_secret_value(
    SecretId="bcbs/research/token-key")["SecretBinary"]

# Safe Harbor: the 18 identifiers. This handles the three that survive
# most naive de-identification attempts.
DIRECT_IDENTIFIERS = [
    "member_name", "street", "phone", "email", "ssn", "mrn",
    "account_number", "certificate_number", "device_id", "url", "ip",
]


def tokenise(value: str) -> str:
    """Stable pseudonym. Same member, same token, across extracts."""
    return hmac.new(_HMAC_KEY, value.encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()[:32]


def shift_date(d: date, member_token: str) -> date:
    """Per-member consistent date shift, +/- 30 days.

    A single global shift is reversible from a known event. A per-member
    shift preserves within-member intervals, which is what researchers
    actually need, without preserving the calendar.
    """
    offset = int(member_token[:4], 16) % 61 - 30
    return date.fromordinal(d.toordinal() + offset)


def deidentify(row: dict) -> dict:
    token = tokenise(row["enterprise_member_id"])
    out = {k: v for k, v in row.items() if k not in DIRECT_IDENTIFIERS}
    out["member_token"] = token
    out.pop("enterprise_member_id", None)

    for field in ("service_date", "admit_date", "discharge_date"):
        if row.get(field):
            out[field] = shift_date(row[field], token)

    # Ages over 89 aggregate to a single bucket under Safe Harbor.
    if row.get("age", 0) > 89:
        out["age"] = "90+"

    # ZIP: first three digits only, and suppressed for sparse areas.
    zip3 = str(row.get("zip", ""))[:3]
    out["zip3"] = "000" if zip3 in SPARSE_ZIP3 else zip3
    return out
SQLThe ELT case — the same logic as a transformation over landed data

Nothing was destroyed on the way in. When the risk adjustment rules change in September you re-run this over history; in the ETL version above, that history is gone.

sql/gold_claim_summary_monthly.sql
-- silver -> gold. Runs over data already landed, re-runnable at will.
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE bcbs_marts.claim_summary_monthly AS
WITH lines AS (
    SELECT
        c.enterprise_member_id,
        c.service_month,
        m.line_of_business,
        c.billed_amount,
        c.allowed_amount,
        c.claim_status
    FROM   bcbs_lakehouse.claim_line c
    JOIN   bcbs_lakehouse.member_month m
      ON   c.enterprise_member_id = m.enterprise_member_id
     AND   c.service_month       = m.service_month
    WHERE  c.is_deleted = false
      AND  c.claim_status IN ('PAID', 'ADJUSTED')
)
SELECT
    enterprise_member_id,
    service_month,
    line_of_business,
    count(*)                   AS line_count,
    sum(billed_amount)         AS billed_amount,
    sum(allowed_amount)        AS allowed_amount,
    current_timestamp          AS built_at
FROM   lines
GROUP  BY 1, 2, 3;

-- The reconciliation that decides whether anyone trusts this table
SELECT  n.service_month,
        n.allowed_amount                       AS new_total,
        l.allowed_amount                       AS legacy_total,
        abs(n.allowed_amount - l.allowed_amount)
          / nullif(l.allowed_amount, 0)        AS variance
FROM   (SELECT service_month, sum(allowed_amount) allowed_amount
        FROM bcbs_marts.claim_summary_monthly GROUP BY 1) n
FULL OUTER JOIN legacy_edw.claim_summary_month l USING (service_month)
WHERE  abs(n.allowed_amount - l.allowed_amount)
         / nullif(l.allowed_amount, 0) > 0.001;   -- 0.1% tolerance

Overlaying Well-Architected

The framework is not a checklist you run at the end. Used properly it is six questions you ask of every design decision, before it is made.

Build it

Ten steps, in dependency order, with deployable code. The accompanying CDK project is the same thing as a repository you can cdk deploy.

Before you run anything. This deploys into an account that must already be covered by a business associate agreement with AWS and configured under your landing zone guardrails. 01Accounts and boundariesBefore a single bucket

Separate accounts for raw, curated and consumption, under an organisational unit with SCPs that no team can lift. The account boundary is the only segmentation that cannot be misconfigured away by a well-meaning engineer.

  • data-raw — bronze only, no human read access, no internet path
  • data-curated — silver and gold, Glue and EMR compute
  • data-consumption — Athena workgroups, Redshift, QuickSight
  • data-governance — Lake Formation administration, DataZone domain, audit
Trap. One account with prefixes for separation. It reads fine in a diagram and gives you nothing: a single over-broad IAM policy exposes every zone at once.
02Keys and the lake bucketsEncryption before data

A customer-managed key per data class, not per bucket. The key policy is where minimum necessary becomes enforceable, because a role without kms:Decrypt cannot read the object no matter what the bucket policy says.

stacks/foundation_stack.py
# One CMK per data class; the key policy is the real control

Gap. Versioning plus a customer-managed key plus seven-year retention is a real cost line. Model it before you turn it on, and put a lifecycle rule to Glacier Instant Retrieval on bronze from day one.

04Streaming ingest — clinical eventsThe only real-time source

ADT messages into a stream, a Lambda that parses and redacts, and a Firehose that keeps the raw message in bronze regardless. Two destinations from one event: the alert path and the history path.

lambda/adt_processor.py
import base64, json, os, boto3

events = boto3.client("events")
BUS = os.environ["EVENT_BUS"]

def handler(event, context):
    for record in event["Records"]:
        raw = base64.b64decode(record["kinesis"]["data"]).decode()
        msg = parse_hl7(raw)                 # MSH, PID, PV1 segments
        if msg["trigger"] not in ("A01", "A03", "A08"):
            continue                         # admit, discharge, update only
        events.put_events(Entries=[{
            "EventBusName": BUS,
            "Source": "bcbs.clinical",
            "DetailType": "AdtEvent",
            # minimum necessary: identifiers and codes, never the note text
            "Detail": json.dumps({
                "member_id": msg["member_id"],
                "trigger": msg["trigger"],
                "facility": msg["facility"],
                "occurred_at": msg["occurred_at"],
            }),
        }])
    return {"processed": len(event["Records"])}
Trap. Putting the whole HL7 message on the event bus because it is convenient. Every downstream subscriber now receives free-text clinical notes it has no right to. Minimum necessary applies to internal fan-out as much as to external sharing.
05CDC from the core platformCurrent state without querying the source

DMS reads the transaction log rather than the tables, so you get changes without load on a system that is adjudicating claims. Land the change events, then merge.

Gap. DMS full-load plus ongoing replication needs a maintenance window and a DBA who trusts you. That conversation takes longer than the build. Start it in week one.
Trap. Treating CDC output as a table. It is an ordered log of changes including deletes. If you append it and query the result you will double-count every corrected claim in the plan.
06Bronze to silver — the job that mattersWhere the real work is

Parse, type, validate, de-duplicate, conform keys, tag PHI columns, write Iceberg. This job is three times the size anyone estimates.

glue/bronze_to_silver_claims.py
from pyspark.sql import functions as F

raw = spark.read.text(f"s3://bcbs-lake-bronze/edi/txn=837/arrival_date={run_date}/")

claims = (
    parse_x12_837(raw)                                # segment parser, tested separately
    .withColumn("ingested_at", F.current_timestamp())
    .withColumn("source_file", F.input_file_name())
    # conform to the enterprise member id, never the payer-assigned one
    .join(member_xref, on="subscriber_id", how="left")
    # de-duplicate on the natural key: a resubmission is not a new claim
    .withColumn("rn", F.row_number().over(
        Window.partitionBy("claim_control_number").orderBy(F.desc("ingested_at"))))
    .filter("rn = 1").drop("rn")
)

# fail the run loudly rather than writing something plausible but wrong
orphans = claims.filter("enterprise_member_id IS NULL").count()
if orphans > threshold:
    raise ValueError(f"{orphans} claims without a member match")

(claims.writeTo("glue_catalog.silver.claim_line")
       .using("iceberg")
       .partitionedBy("service_month")
       .createOrReplace())
Trap. Silently dropping unmatched rows. A claim without a member match is a data problem someone must see. Quarantine it to a table with an owner and an alert; never let it vanish into a filter.
07Orchestration and quality gatesFailure has to be loud

Step Functions rather than cron. Every stage has an explicit failure path, and a data quality gate sits between silver and gold so a bad load cannot reach a business report.

stacks/process_stack.py
quality_gate = tasks.CallAwsService(
    self, "RunDataQuality",
    service="glue", action="startDataQualityRulesetEvaluationRun",
    parameters={"RulesetNames": ["claim_line_rules"], ...},
)

definition = (
    load_bronze_to_silver
    .next(quality_gate)

Rules worth having on day one: row count within a band of the trailing average, no nulls in the natural key, referential integrity to member and provider, and freshness — the newest service date must be within an expected window of the run date.

Trap. Quality rules that only alert. If a failing load still lands in gold, the rule is documentation. Stop the pipeline and page the steward.

Register the buckets with Lake Formation, create the tag taxonomy, and grant on tags. Do this before the second table exists.

09Serving layerChosen per query pattern

Athena for exploration and anything infrequent. Redshift where a known join pattern runs constantly and concurrency matters — actuarial and finance usually justify it, ad hoc analytics usually do not. The CMS Patient Access API serves from a purpose-built store, never from the lake.

Gap. The regulated APIs have latency and availability expectations that a lakehouse does not meet. Budget for a separate serving store for anything a member or regulator hits directly.

The one that ends programmes

Reconciliation. The day someone runs the old report and the new one and gets different numbers, every argument about architecture stops and the platform is on trial. Build reconciliation against the existing warehouse into the definition of done for every gold table — not as a phase at the end.

Interview

Enterprise architecture interviews are not knowledge tests. They are watching how you reason under incomplete information, and whether you can hold a position without being rigid about it.

The structure that works for any question

Clarify what decision the thing supports — constrain by naming the two or three things that actually bind — choose and say why — concede the cost of choosing it — check what you would measure to know you were wrong.

Ten seconds of clarifying is worth two minutes of answering the wrong question. Interviewers grade the clarifying.

ARCHITECTURE JUDGEMENT

The questions you will actually get

QUESTIONWalk me through how you would design our claims data platform.
The answer that lands. Do not start drawing. Ask three things: what decisions does the business make on claims data and how often; where does the data come from and in what form; and what is already in place that we cannot switch off. Then draw the five stages, name the three zones and their contracts, and say explicitly that governance and security are overlays rather than a phase. Finish with the sequence — batch first because it serves most of the value, streaming last because only a few use cases justify it.
What sinks it. Opening with a technology list. The moment you say Kinesis before you say what decision expires, you have told them you design from the tooling up.

Follow-up they will ask: why medallion rather than a data vault or a warehouse? Answer: medallion is a contract about mutability, not a modelling technique. You can put a vault or a star inside gold. What medallion buys you is an immutable replay source, which is what lets you reprocess when a risk adjustment rule changes retroactively.

QUESTIONHow do you decide between streaming and batch?
The answer that lands. By asking what the consumer does in the gap. If a nightly refresh means someone acts at nine in the morning on something they would have acted on at nine anyway, there is no streaming requirement — there is a habit. Streaming is justified when the decision expires: a care-gap alert after discharge is worthless. I would also point out that the source often decides for you. An 837 file drop is a batch whatever you wrap around it.
What sinks it. Saying "real time is always better". It is a cost and complexity multiplier and it makes replay harder, and an interviewer who has run a platform knows that.
QUESTIONETL or ELT? Defend your choice.
The answer that lands. ELT by default, because storage is cheaper than reacquiring data and because I will need to reprocess history when a rule changes — which in risk adjustment and quality happens constantly. ETL only where an obligation says a form of the data must not exist at rest: de-identification before it crosses into a research boundary, or a contract forbidding retention of the source payload. Then I do that transformation as early in the path as the obligation demands, and I record which clause required it.
What sinks it. Framing it as a trend. "ELT is the modern approach" is not an argument. The argument is reprocessing and lineage.
QUESTIONYou have inherited a warehouse everyone hates and a two-year mandate. What do you do first?
The answer that lands. Not turn it off. I would find the twenty reports that actually get read, trace their lineage back, and build gold tables that reconcile to them within a tolerance the finance team agrees to. Run both estates until reconciliation passes for two consecutive cycles. The programme is judged on whether the numbers tie out, not on whether the lakehouse is elegant — and the day someone runs both reports and gets different answers is the day the architecture goes on trial.
What sinks it. A big-bang migration plan with a cutover date. Everyone in the room has seen one of those fail.
QUESTIONHow do you handle PHI in a data lake?
The answer that lands. Three things, in order. Classification first, because handling is derived from the class rather than negotiated per project. Then the key policy, because a role without kms:Decrypt cannot read the object whatever the bucket policy says. Then Lake Formation tag-based access with column exclusion and row filters, because that is how minimum necessary becomes evidence rather than intention. And I would say plainly that bronze holds PHI in its rawest form, so no human principal is ever granted on the bronze zone tag.
What sinks it. Saying "we encrypt everything". Everyone encrypts everything. The question is who holds decrypt, and how you prove minimum necessary to an auditor.
QUESTIONWhat is the hardest part of this build?
The answer that lands. Silver, and it is the layer every estimate underestimates by about three times. Bronze is a landing job. Gold is SQL. Silver is where you conform member identifiers across lines of business, de-duplicate resubmissions on the natural key, decide what a corrected claim means, and handle late arrivals. The second hardest is organisational: clinical data usually has no steward, and until someone owns the golden record every clinical design decision gets made by whoever asked most recently.
What sinks it. Naming a technology as the hard part. Nobody senior believes the hard part was choosing a query engine.
QUESTIONHow would you use the Well-Architected Framework here?
The answer that lands. As six questions asked before a design is built, not as a review at the end. For this platform, security asks who can read PHI and how minimum necessary is evidenced; reliability asks what happens when a partner sends nothing on Tuesday and then sends it twice on Wednesday. I would also use the Data Analytics and Healthcare lenses, because the base framework will not ask about de-identification methodology or PHI in intermediate storage, and those are the two findings that matter most for a payer.
What sinks it. Treating it as a checklist. If your answer is "we did a WAR and closed the findings", you have described an audit, not an architecture practice.
QUESTIONThe CFO asks why this costs fifteen thousand a month. What do you say?
The answer that lands. I break it down and lead with the largest line, which is almost always Redshift at around half. Then I answer the question actually being asked, which is marginal cost: what does one more data product cost? Storage plus the Glue hours to build it plus the scan its consumers run. And I would bring one number showing what came off the other side of the ledger — the reports retired, the extracts no longer hand-built, the hours the actuarial team stopped spending reconciling spreadsheets.
What sinks it. Defending the total. Nobody is asking you to justify the platform; they are asking whether the next thing is affordable.
QUESTIONHow do you get an application team to adopt your reference architecture?
The answer that lands. By making the compliant path the fast one. A reference architecture with no module, template or pipeline behind it is a slide, and teams route around slides. I would ship the account vending, the golden pipeline and the module library first, and put the guardrail behind them in warn mode with a published enforcement date. Enforcement before paving is what creates a permanent exception queue, and the exception queue then becomes the real architecture.
What sinks it. Governance-first answers. "I would take it to the architecture review board" tells them you expect compliance rather than adoption.
QUESTIONTell me about an architecture decision you got wrong.
The answer that lands. Pick a real one where the cost was visible and the lesson was structural, not personal. The shape that works: what you chose, the constraint you underweighted, how you found out, what you changed, and what you now ask earlier because of it. The best answers end with a question that is now permanently on your checklist.
What sinks it. A humblebrag reversal, or a decision that turned out fine. They are testing whether you can hold a mistake without either minimising it or performing contrition.
THE WHITEBOARD

How to run the design exercise

The first five minutes

  • Write the question at the top of the board in your own words. Get agreement before you draw.
  • List what you are told, what you are assuming, and what you would go and find out. Keep all three visible.
  • Name the two or three constraints that actually bind. Everything else is detail.
  • Sketch the boxes before any service names. Service names are the last thing you write.

Signals they are grading

  • Did you ask what decision the data supports, or start with the pipeline?
  • Did you name a trade-off unprompted, or wait to be caught?
  • Did you say what you would measure to learn you were wrong?
  • When challenged, did you defend or did you update? Both are wrong at the extremes.
TURNING IT ROUND

What to ask them

Three questions that tell you more about the role than the job description does — and signal seniority while you ask.

Ask thisWhat the answer tells you
Who owns the clinical data domain today?If nobody, you now know your first six months. If the answer is a committee, worse.

What was the last architecture decision that got reversed, and why?Whether decisions are recorded, and whether the organisation can change its mind without blame.

Practice

Reading an architecture does not make you able to produce one under pressure. These are drills — do them on paper, timed, without looking anything up.

DRILLS

Five exercises, in order of difficulty

DRILL1 · The fifteen-minute lane assignment

For each use case below, write the pattern, the AWS services and the single reason. Fifteen minutes total, no more than ninety seconds each.

  1. Provider directory accuracy check against the NPI registry, weekly
  2. Member calls the service desk and the agent needs current claim status
  3. Fraud model scores a claim before it is released for payment
  4. Price transparency machine-readable file, monthly, 40 GB output
  5. Prior authorisation decision must be returned inside a regulated clock
  6. Annual HEDIS hybrid chart review sample selection
  7. Member opens the app and sees their explanation of benefits
  8. Actuarial trend analysis over three years of claims

Mark yourself: two of the eight are traps. Items 2 and 7 look like streaming and are not — 2 is CDC into a serving store, 7 is a read from gold through an API with its own store. If you put Kinesis on either, you have designed for the verb rather than the decision.

DRILL2 · Write a zone contract from memory

Blank sheet. Draw the three-column table and fill in seven rows: what it holds, format, partitioned by, who may read, quality gate, what you do with a defect, retention. Twelve minutes.

The two rows people get wrong: partitioning (arrival in bronze, business date in silver — and being able to say why in one sentence) and defect handling (never edit bronze, reprocess forward). If you can produce this table cold you can hold your own in any lakehouse design discussion.

DRILL3 · The reconciliation conversation

Role-play, out loud, five minutes. Finance says the new claim mart shows 0.4% less allowed amount than the legacy warehouse for March. Your pipeline is correct. Explain what you do.

What a strong answer contains. You do not defend the pipeline. You decompose the variance: is it a row-count difference or an amount difference; is it concentrated in one line of business, one month, one claim type; does the legacy report include voided claims that you exclude; is the service-month boundary handled differently. You name the most likely cause first — usually the treatment of adjustments and voids — and you agree a tolerance with finance rather than arguing about who is right. Then you write it into the data contract so the same conversation never happens twice.
DRILL4 · Design a de-identification approach in twenty minutes

A research partner wants three years of claims and clinical data. Produce: the method you would use and why, what happens to dates, what happens to ZIP codes and ages, how you keep a member linkable across extracts without being identifiable, who signs it off, and what you would put in the data use agreement.

The two things that separate a good answer: a per-member consistent date shift rather than a global one, because a global shift is reversible from any known event and a per-member shift still preserves the within-member intervals researchers need. And knowing that Safe Harbor and Expert Determination are different routes with different consequences — the first is a checklist, the second is a documented statistical opinion that lets you retain more.

DRILL5 · The ninety-minute full exercise

Set a timer. Produce, on paper, a complete design for one of these. Then leave it a day and mark it against the traps table.

  1. CMS Patient Access API. Members can pull their claims and clinical data through a third-party app. Design the serving path, the identity model, the throttling, and where the data comes from. Note that serving this from the lake is the trap.
  2. Real-time fraud hold. Score a claim before payment release without adding more than 400ms to adjudication. Where does the model live, how does it get features, and what happens when it is unavailable?
  3. Payer-to-payer data exchange. A member switches plans and their prior plan must hand over five years of data. Design the exchange, the consent model, and the reconciliation.

Mark against: did you name the steward for every dataset; did you say what happens when the source is late, duplicated or absent; did you state a latency requirement and justify it with a decision; did you say what you would measure to know it was working; did you write down what you deliberately did not build.

WHAT TO BUILD NEXT

If you want one more thing on your desk

The X12 parser

The single highest-value thing you can build and test properly. Recorded partner files, unit tests per segment, and a golden-file suite that fails when a partner changes something without telling you.

The reconciliation harness

Automated variance reporting between every gold table and its legacy equivalent. This is what turns "do you trust it" from an opinion into a number, and it is what gets the old warehouse switched off.

The decision log

Twenty ADRs covering the choices already made before you arrived, reconstructed by interviewing whoever remembers. Tedious, unglamorous, and the thing that stops the next two years being spent relitigating.

The habit that makes an architect

Write down what you decided, what you ruled out, and what would change your mind — every time, even when it feels obvious. The value is not in the document. It is that writing the third line forces you to know whether you have a reason or a preference.

Most architecture failures are preferences that were never tested because nobody wrote them down as claims.
