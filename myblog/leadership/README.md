# What kind of leader are you, actually?

Twenty-two moments, all on one page, with a read on each answer as you give it.
About six minutes.

One HTML file. No build, no dependencies, no server, and **nothing stored anywhere
at all** — not even in your own browser.

---

## Put it online in two steps

1. **Make a repo** and upload `index.html`, `README.md` and the `.github` folder.
2. **Settings → Pages → Source: _GitHub Actions_.**

It publishes to `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo-name>/` in about a minute
and republishes on every push to `main`. There is nothing to configure.

---

## What you get

**A name for your pattern.** One of twenty-three — Micromanager, Commander, Nice One,
Postponer, Spotlight Taker, Scar Leaver, Self-Preserver, Favouritist and the rest.
None is a compliment and none is an insult; each names a real strength and the
thing that strength costs.

**A straight answer to whether they would work for you again.** Five bands, from
*they would follow you to your next job* down to *they think about you outside
work, and not fondly.*

**Small steps.** Three graded actions — this week, this month, this quarter — for
each of your three furthest-out traits. Deliberately small: one visible change
beats five invisible intentions.

**Who thrives under you, and who quietly does not.** For each of your furthest-out
traits: the people who do well with a manager like you, the people who are paying
for it without saying so, and what to do about it when hiring or reshaping a team.

**What to tell your team, up front.** Eleven first-person sentences, one per trait,
written from where you actually sit — ready to say out loud or paste into a
document. Most of the damage a leadership style does comes from people working it
out by trial and error; saying it on day one removes about half of that.

**A quadrant** on your result and again on All patterns, where all twenty-three
single-trait patterns are plotted as round dots and four worked composites — one
per corner — as dark diamonds. A single trait pushed to an extreme almost never
lands you in Visionaries or Niche players; those take a combination, so the corners
get real examples rather than forced dots. Plotted on Blake and Mouton's axes alongside the aim zone and your own
position.

**All eleven spectrums against what good looks like, where it ends up if
nothing changes, and what your team gets and pays for every trait.**

## The PDF

**Download the PDF** builds a real PDF in the page — no library, no service, no
upload. It contains your name for it, your pattern, the verdict, every spectrum
drawn with its healthy band, all the small steps, the timeline, and **all twenty
answers with the read on each one.** Five A4 pages, and it is the only copy that
survives closing the tab.

## Working through the questions

The five sections fold. Click any header to collapse it, or use expand and collapse
all. Tick **fold a section when it is finished** and each one closes itself as you
complete it, so what is left is always what you can see.

They also sort four ways: in order, unanswered first, answered first, or A to Z.
Unanswered first is the useful one when you come back to a half-finished set. Each
header carries its own small progress bar and count.

## Seeing a worked example first

The Answer tab offers one before you start: a delivery-focused leader with a
people-side gap, three anonymous replies already in, and every section of the
result filled in. Clearing it puts you back at a blank set, and answering any
question clears it automatically — the example can never be mistaken for your own
result, and its PDF says so on the first line.

## All patterns

Opens with a four-number summary, then the quadrant, then all twenty-three
patterns grouped by trait — and closes with a histogram of how leaders are
actually distributed in companies, with its own sources underneath.

That last part is real-world data rather than anything this tool produces: about
1 in 10 people have high natural talent to manage and a further 2 in 10 reach a
high standard with investment; global engagement sits at 20%; trust in immediate
managers has fallen to 29%; and manager appointments pick the wrong candidate
about 82% of the time. Gallup and DDI, cited in full at the bottom of the tab.

## Compatibility zones

Eight kinds of person — Self-Starter, Apprentice, Craftsperson, Climber, Steady
Operator, Challenger, Quiet One, Anxious High Performer — each scored against what
they actually need from a manager, given your profile. Four bands: strong fit,
workable, friction, poor fit.

Each row opens to show what works, where the friction is (naming the single trait
doing the damage), and what to do with that person specifically.

Not a hiring filter. A team made only of your top two rows would be very
comfortable and would never argue with you. The point is knowing where the friction
sits before somebody works it out alone and leaves.

The score is not a plain average — failing the thing that matters most to someone
is not rescued by being fine on the rest. An anxious high performer under a
volatile manager is not saved by good candour.

appear on the Answer tab and on your result, and both say exactly what goes before

## The Simulation tab

Twenty thousand synthetic leaders answer the same twenty-two moments, scored by
exactly the functions that scored you, run in your browser the moment you open the
tab. Nothing is precomputed — change a question and the distribution changes with
it.

Three ways for the simulated leaders to answer: at random, realistically, or kind
to themselves. Every pattern is listed by how often it comes up, with a small
spectrum showing where it sits on its own trait, and each row opens to show what
that pattern does to a team, who thrives under it, who struggles, and the line
that leader could say up front.

It is a property test of the instrument rather than evidence about real managers.
It found a genuine defect during development: four traits fed only the delivery
axis, so a volatile micromanager could score 84 out of 100 on whether people would
work for them again. Every trait now feeds both axes with a weight.

## Everything folds

Every panel with a heading collapses, on every tab — result sections, the
simulation, the pattern library and its groups, the question sections. Each tab
with more than one panel gets an expand-all / collapse-all bar at the top. Pattern
cards start closed so the library is scannable, except your own, which opens.

## Nothing is stored

No localStorage, no cookies, no sessionStorage, no server, no analytics. Nothing
carries from one visit to the next. Answers exist in the page you have open and
nowhere else — refresh and they are gone, which is why the page warns you at the
start and asks before you navigate away.

The one exception is deliberate and yours: **Download this page** writes the answers
into the saved file, so a copy you chose to keep reopens as itself rather than
blank. That is a document, not a session.

---

## Asking your team, anonymously

Put your name in and you get a link to send. They answer the same twenty-two moments
about you and get a short code back — **twenty-two numbers and nothing else.** No name,
no timestamp, nothing that identifies who sent it.

**Nothing from your team appears until three codes have been added.** With one or
two replies you could work out who wrote what, and that possibility is the reason
people answer carefully rather than honestly.

Two things worth saying out loud when you send it:

**It is optional.** Someone deciding not to answer is a data point, and not one you
should chase.

**You are not going to argue with it.** If you do, once, you will never get an
honest answer from that person again, and they will tell the others.

## The eleven traits

Both ends of every one of these is a real way to fail.

| | One end | The other |
|---|---|---|
| **Grip** | Hands off | Hands on |
| **Command** | Laissez-faire | Militaristic |
| **Candour** | Just nice | Blunt |
| **Conflict** | Lets it run | Steps in fast |
| **Growth** | All of them | All of you |
| **Spotlight** | Gives it away | Takes it |
| **Weather** | Flat calm | Storm front |
| **Wake** | Leaves scars | Creates dependence |
| **Self-interest** | Never yourself | Yourself first |
| **Outlook** | Nothing is good enough | Everything is fine |
| **Even-handed** | Over-corrects | Has favourites |

**The worst trait carries most of the weight.** The people axis is not an average —
nobody stays for a manager who is excellent at five things and leaves scars on the
sixth.

**The healthy bands are not centred.** On candour, conflict and wake, erring past
the middle beats falling short of it. On the rest it does not. The References tab
says where each position comes from.

## Making it yours

Everything is in one file. Moments are in `Q`, each option carrying its own one-line
read. Traits are in `AX`, patterns and timelines in `TYPE`, team impact in `READ`,
the graded actions in `STEPS`, team fit in `FIT`, the up-front lines in `MANUAL`, sources in `REFS`, and the PDF writer in `PDF()`.
