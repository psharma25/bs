1. Purpose

This addendum sets out how PromptHalo Technologies (“PromptHalo” or “the Company”) evaluates cyber liability insurance as a method of transferring information-security and business risk, and how the resulting treatment decision is approved, documented, and revisited. It supplements — and does not replace — the Risk Management Policy.

2. Scope

This addendum applies to all information-security and business risks that carry a financial loss component, including risks arising from security incidents, unauthorized disclosure of customer or personal data, business interruption, and third-party liability. It applies to the Company as a whole and to all systems, services, and data within the scope of the Risk Management Policy.

3. Policy Statement

PromptHalo evaluates cyber liability insurance as one potential method of transferring information-security and business risks. Insurance is treated as a risk-transfer option alongside — not as a substitute for — the Company’s preventive and detective controls.

4. Evaluation

4.1 Cadence

At least annually, and following material changes to the Company’s operations, customer obligations, data processing activities, or risk exposure, management will assess the need, availability, scope, and cost-effectiveness of cyber insurance.

4.2 Factors considered

The evaluation takes into account, at minimum:

  • Organizational size and financial resources, including the Company’s capacity to absorb a loss.
  • Contractual obligations, including any customer or partner commitments that specify coverage or minimum limits.
  • Data exposure — the volume, sensitivity, and regulatory character of the data PromptHalo processes.
  • Existing preventive and detective controls and their effect on the likelihood and impact of a loss event.
  • Availability, scope, exclusions, and cost-effectiveness of coverage in the current market.
  • Applicable legal and regulatory requirements.

5. Risk Acceptance

Based on PromptHalo’s current organizational size, financial resources, contractual obligations, data exposure, and existing preventive and detective controls, management may elect to accept the residual financial risk rather than purchase cyber insurance.

Where management so elects, the decision, rationale, approving authority, and next review date will be documented in the Company’s risk register. The risk register entry will record the following:

FieldRecorded value
Risk IDReference to the parent risk entry (e.g., RSK-###)
Risk descriptionFinancial loss arising from a security incident, data breach, or business interruption not offset by insurance
Treatment decisionAccept / Transfer / Mitigate / Avoid
RationaleBasis for the decision, including the factors listed in Section 4.2
Approving authorityName and title of the approver
Approval dateDate the decision was approved
Next review dateNo later than twelve months from the approval date

An election to accept residual financial risk does not reduce any obligation under the Risk Management Policy to identify, assess, and treat the underlying risk through other means.

6. Reconsideration Triggers

Cyber insurance will be reconsidered when required by contract, law, customer commitment, or when management determines that the Company’s risk exposure justifies transferring the risk. Events that trigger reconsideration ahead of the annual review include:

  • Execution of, or a proposed amendment to, a customer or partner agreement that requires cyber liability coverage.
  • A new legal or regulatory requirement for coverage in a market PromptHalo serves.
  • A material change in the volume, sensitivity, or regulatory character of data processed.
  • A material change in the Company’s size, funding, revenue, or service offering.
  • A security incident, near-miss, or audit finding that materially changes the assessed exposure.

9. Exceptions

Exceptions to this addendum require written approval from the approving authority identified above and must be recorded in the risk register with a defined expiration or review date.

10. Review

This addendum is reviewed at least annually, or upon a triggering event described in Section 6, by the Security Officer and approved by the approving authority.
