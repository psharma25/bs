How a score is produced

Every score in this register is arithmetic: likelihood × impact, each on a 1–5 anchored scale, giving 1–25. Nothing is estimated by the tool. Two assessors given the same anchors should land on the same number, and where they don't, the disagreement is about the anchor, which is a conversation worth having.

Inherent is scored assuming the controls listed against the risk are absent or wholly ineffective. Residual is scored on the controls as they actually operate today, not as designed and not as intended. The difference between the two is the measured value of the control set, and it is the number a board should be asking about.

The common failure. Teams score inherent risk as "what it would be without any controls at all", which drives every inherent score to 20–25 and makes the column meaningless. Score inherent against the specific controls named on that row being removed, and the column starts carrying information.

Appetite and escalation

The appetite line sits at a residual score of 9. Residual at 10 or above requires either an active treatment plan with a named owner and a date, or a formal acceptance recorded against the risk with the accepting authority named. Residual at 16 or above is reported to the steering committee at every sitting until it comes down, regardless of treatment progress.

Event triggers that force an out-of-cycle re-score

A register that only moves on the calendar is a document. A register that moves on events is a control. Re-score on: a security incident touching the risk; a new or changed system, vendor, or data flow; an audit or assessment finding; a material change in the threat picture for the asset class; a control failure or a missed control test; a change of owner; and any acceptance reaching its expiry date.

Framework mapping

Control references on each row point at the control library, not at framework text. One control can satisfy several frameworks, so mapping is held once on the control and inherited by every risk that cites it. That is what keeps the register from forking into a SOC 2 register, an ISO register, and a customer-questionnaire register that quietly disagree with one another.
