What the platform does, for whom, and across which trust boundaries. The customer configures a target, Litmus generates adversarial traffic from AWS and fires it at that target, scores the responses, and returns findings, a report and notifications. The loop closes when the team re-scans.

The open question is runbook coverage for a scan that hangs past the visibility timeout.
SEC
SecurityNo public IPs on any task, one ingress point, one egress point, secrets only from SSM and Secrets Manager, no static CI credentials, KMS everywhere, hashed API keys. The gaps are listed in the security overlay.
REL
Reliabilitytask_acks_late with prefetch of one, a dedicated notifications queue so long scan monitors cannot starve short tasks, ALB health checks deregistering unhealthy targets, and SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED on schedule dispatch. Single-NAT and single-AZ exposure is worth confirming.
PERF
Performance efficiencyMoving Deep scan inference from CPU Ollama to Bedrock took a scan from three or four hours to about fifteen minutes. Basic and Deep run as parallel tasks rather than in sequence.
COST
Cost optimisationGateway and interface endpoints keep S3, ECR and SSM traffic off the NAT. Fargate tasks are ephemeral, so scan compute is only paid for while a scan runs. The unconfigured Bedrock endpoint is the one visible cost leak.
SUS
SustainabilityNothing idles for scan capacity. Tasks are created per scan and exit at completion, and the container images are shared across services rather than duplicated.

Model

How a tenant is created for each customer

What actually happens between a signup form and a first scan, what exists per customer once it has, and where the boundary between two customers really sits. The short answer is that it sits in one place: the organisation identifier carried in the token and applied to every query.

How a tenant gets created10 steps, signup to first scan
1
Signup, or an invite from an existing tenantA Cloudflare Turnstile challenge is validated on the server before anything is created. Nothing is written until that passes.
2
Auth0 user and Auth0 Organization createdThe backend calls the Auth0 Management API through the NAT. Auth0 mints the organisation identifier that becomes the tenant's identity for the life of the account.
3
organizations and users rows written to RDSThe Auth0 identity is synced into the platform's own database. Social sign-in skips the signup form, so an onboarding step collects the company name afterwards and PATCHes it in.
4
The tenant identity enters every tokenFrom here the JWT carries sub, org_id, role, tier and permissions. Every later request resolves through AuthContext, and org_id is what scopes the query.
5
Stripe customer and subscription createdbilling_accounts and subscriptions rows link the tenant to its plan. The tier is what the entitlement check reads when a scan is requested.
6
An S3 prefix is reservedResults land under {client_id}/ in the shared bucket. The prefix is the isolation boundary for objects, enforced by application logic rather than by a bucket policy.
7
Quotas and platform defaults appliedScan quota and tier limits come from the subscription; PAIR iteration caps and worker settings come from the single global platform_config row shared by all tenants.
8
API keys issued on requestckm_ prefixed keys for CI and integrations, stored hashed. The key resolves to the same org_id as a session token would.
9
Users invited and roles assignedThe tenant administrator adds members through user management. Roles ride in the token as permissions rather than being looked up per request.
10
Every subsequent read and write is scopedScans, findings, reports, webhooks, schedules and prompt libraries all filter on the organisation. One control, applied consistently, is the whole isolation model.

How to harden the model6 recommendations
  • This is a pooled model: one database, one bucket, one set of keys, with isolation enforced in application code. That is a normal and defensible choice at this stage, but it should be stated plainly in a security questionnaire rather than implied.
  • Because org_id in AuthContext is the only thing standing between tenants, it deserves a dedicated negative test suite that runs in CI: a cross-tenant scan_id, report_id, share token, webhook and S3 key, each expected to fail.
  • Postgres row-level security would move the isolation from every query to the database itself, so a forgotten filter fails closed instead of leaking. It is the highest-value change on this list.
  • An IAM condition on the S3 prefix, or a scan task role scoped to one client_id, would stop a compromised scan container from reading another tenant's reports. Today the task role can reach the whole bucket.
  • A per-tenant KMS key is the answer to the enterprise question about cryptographic separation, and it also gives a clean, provable deletion story: destroy the key and the data is unrecoverable regardless of what is left in backups.
  • For a customer who will not accept a pooled model at all, the fallback is a silo deployment in its own account. Price it deliberately rather than discovering the requirement in a negotiation.

Security components still missing, and what they cost

The architecture is well built for what it does, but it is thin on the components that let you see, contain and prove. This is what I would add, in the order I would add it, with indicative monthly cost so the conversation is about sequencing rather than about whether. Roughly 950 to 2,700 US dollars a month for the paid route, though about two thirds of it has a credible free or open-source alternative, listed against each component, against a platform whose Bedrock and Fargate bill already moves with scan volume.

What these numbers assume5 notes
  • Figures are AWS list price in us-east-1, rounded to indicative monthly ranges, and assume a single region, roughly fifty to two hundred scans a day, under a terabyte of monthly egress and a small team. They are order-of-magnitude planning numbers, not a quote.
  • Costs that scale with volume, particularly GuardDuty, flow logs, Macie and the SIEM store, will move with scan volume rather than with customer count. Model them per scan, not per tenant.
  • The Bedrock interface endpoint and the S3 lifecycle rules both reduce spend. They are the two items on this list that pay for themselves.
  • Phase 1 and Phase 2 together are roughly the cost of a single mid-size Fargate service running continuously. Set against one enterprise medtech deal, the whole programme is noise; set against a failed security review, it is the cheapest insurance on the list.
  • Nothing here is a substitute for the four open items in the security overlay. Tooling detects; those are design decisions that have to be made regardless of what you buy.

Key

Glossary

Plain definitions for the terms used across the three views, grouped the way the diagrams are grouped. Written for a reader who has to sign off on this without being an AWS engineer.

Network and delivery10 terms
ALB, Application Load Balancer
Layer 7 load balancer. The only thing in the account that accepts inbound traffic from the internet. It ends TLS and forwards plain HTTP to the application.
ACM, AWS Certificate Manager
Issues and renews the TLS certificate the load balancer presents. Renewal is automatic, so there is no manual certificate rotation to forget.
NAT Gateway
Lets resources in a private subnet reach the internet without being reachable from it. Everything outbound is rewritten to come from one address.
Elastic IP
A fixed public address. Because all egress leaves through one, a customer can allowlist a single address for the scanner.
Internet Gateway
The VPC component that actually moves packets between the VPC and the internet. The NAT Gateway routes through it.
VPC endpoint
A private door onto an AWS service. Traffic to S3, ECR and SSM reaches them without touching the internet or the NAT, and without a per-gigabyte charge.
Public / private subnet
Public subnets have a route to the internet gateway. Private ones do not, which is why no scan task can be reached from outside.
Security group
A stateful allow-list on a resource. The ALB group accepts 443 from anywhere and only talks onward to the application group on port 8000.
CloudFront and Cloudflare
Two content delivery layers in front of the single-page app. They serve the static bundle; neither is in the path of an API call.
SNAT, source network address translation
Rewriting the sender address on the way out. It is why every probe appears to the customer to come from one address.
Compute and orchestration10 terms
ECS, Elastic Container Service
AWS container orchestrator. It decides what container runs where and keeps services at their desired count.
Fargate
Serverless compute for containers. There is no EC2 instance to patch or scale, and a task is billed only while it runs.
Task definition
The blueprint for a container: image, CPU and memory, environment variables and IAM role. checkmate-scan and da3lab-scan are two of them.
RunTask
The API call that launches one container from a task definition. Used here to start a scan, rather than keeping a scan worker warm.
Service versus task
A service keeps containers running continuously, like the API. A task runs once and exits, like a scan.
Celery
A Python job queue. Work that outlives an HTTP request, such as watching a scan to completion, is handed to a Celery worker.
Celery Beat
The scheduler that fires recurring jobs, here dispatching due scan schedules once a minute.
Visibility timeout
How long the broker waits before assuming a job was lost and handing it to another worker. It must exceed the longest scan, hence 330 minutes.
task_acks_late and prefetch
Acknowledge a job after it finishes rather than when it is picked up, and hold only one at a time. Together they stop a crash losing work or a long job starving short ones.
SELECT FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
A database pattern that lets several workers claim rows without two of them claiming the same one. It is what stops a schedule firing twice.
Data and storage8 terms
RDS
Managed relational database. Here PostgreSQL, in a private subnet, reachable only from the application subnet.
ElastiCache Redis
Managed in-memory store. Used as the job broker, the short-lived result store and the browser session map.
S3 and the bucket policy
Object storage. The policy here refuses any request that is not over HTTPS, so an accidental plain HTTP read fails rather than succeeds quietly.
SSE-KMS
Server-side encryption where the key is managed in KMS rather than by S3 alone, so key use is auditable and revocable.
KMS, Key Management Service
Where the encryption keys live. S3, RDS, ElastiCache and every SecureString parameter sit behind it.
Pre-signed URL
A time-limited link that carries its own authorisation. Anyone holding it can read the object until it expires, with no login.
Share token
A no-authentication read link the platform issues for a report. Like a pre-signed URL, possession is permission.
PHI, protected health information
Health data about an identifiable person. Litmus does not seek it, but captured model responses can contain it if the target is a clinical assistant.
Data protection roles and terms17 terms
Controller
The party that decides why and how personal data is processed. Your customer is the controller for the data their scans touch, because they chose the target and the purpose.
Processor
A party that processes personal data on the controller's instructions and for no purpose of its own. Litmus is a processor to its customers.
Subprocessor
A processor engaged by another processor. Every third party in the register that touches customer data is a subprocessor of Litmus, and each one has to be disclosed to the customer and covered by contract.
Independent controller
A party that decides its own purposes for the same data rather than following instructions. Stripe is an independent controller for payment data, which is why its retention clock is longer than the platform's and correctly so.
Joint controllers
Two parties that jointly decide purpose and means, and are jointly answerable to the individual. Rare in a scanning product, but worth ruling out explicitly rather than assuming.
Data processing agreement
The contract that binds a processor: what it may do, which subprocessors it uses, how it handles deletion, breach notification and audit. This is the document the vendor register and the retention schedule feed into.
Subprocessor change notice
The commitment to tell customers before adding or swapping a subprocessor, and to let them object. It is the reason an unclassified party in the delivery chain is a contractual problem, not only a security one.
Covered entity
Under HIPAA, a health plan, clearinghouse or provider. A covered entity's obligations flow down to anyone handling protected health information on its behalf.
Business associate and BAA
A business associate handles protected health information for a covered entity, under a business associate agreement. If a hospital or device manufacturer runs scans whose evidence captures PHI, this is the contract that has to exist first.
Data subject
The identifiable person the data is about. In a red-teaming platform they are usually not the customer's user but whoever appears inside a captured model response, which is what makes the evidence store sensitive.
Personal data and PHI
Personal data is anything identifying a person. Protected health information is personal data tied to health, care or payment for care, and carries a stricter regime. The platform can acquire both without ever asking for either.
Data residency
The commitment about which countries or regions data is stored and processed in. Cross-region model inference is the most common way a single-region promise quietly becomes untrue.
Cross-border transfer and SCCs
Moving personal data outside its home jurisdiction, which needs a legal mechanism such as standard contractual clauses. Relevant here because model inference and edge delivery are both global by default.
Storage limitation
The principle that data is kept no longer than the purpose needs. It is why a retention schedule is a control rather than a housekeeping preference, and why the shortest defensible period beats the most convenient one.
Right to erasure
An individual's right to have their data deleted. The practical hard case is backups: deletion completes when the backup window rolls, which is why that window needs to be short enough to state out loud.
Record of processing activities
The inventory of what personal data is processed, why, on what basis, with whom it is shared and how long it is kept. The data overlay, the vendor register and the retention schedule are three views of it.
Data protection impact assessment
A structured assessment required when processing is likely to be high risk. Capturing verbatim model output that can contain health data is a strong candidate for one.
Identity and secrets11 terms
Auth0
The identity provider. It authenticates users and issues tokens, so no password ever reaches the platform.
JWT, JSON Web Token
A signed token carrying who the user is, which organisation they belong to and what they may do. It is presented on every API call.
RS256
Asymmetric signing. Auth0 holds the private key and the platform verifies with the public one, so a breach of the platform cannot forge tokens.
JWKS
The published set of public keys used to verify those signatures. The API fetches and caches it.
AuthContext
The object built after a token is verified, carrying the organisation, role and permissions. Every authorisation decision resolves through it.
Tenant isolation
Keeping one customer's data unreachable by another. Here it depends on the organisation identifier inside AuthContext being applied to every query.
Turnstile
Cloudflare's bot challenge, validated on the server at signup before any account is created.
Secrets Manager and SSM Parameter Store
Where credentials and configuration live. Values are injected into a container at start by its IAM role, so no human handles them and no image contains them.
IAM task role
The identity a container assumes. It grants exactly the AWS actions that container needs and nothing else.
OIDC and STS
How the build pipeline authenticates without stored keys: it presents a signed identity claim and receives short-lived credentials in exchange.
SigV4
AWS request signing. It proves who made an API call and that the request was not altered in flight.
Red teaming21 terms
Red teaming
Attacking your own system deliberately to find what an adversary would find first.
Probe
One crafted input sent to the target to test for a specific weakness.
Adapter
The translation layer between the probe runner and whatever shape the customer's endpoint takes: OpenAI-compatible, Anthropic, plain HTTP, a browser, and others.
Prompt injection
Getting a model to follow instructions hidden in its input rather than the ones its operator intended.
Jailbreak
Getting a model to produce output its guardrails were meant to refuse.
Data exfiltration
Getting a model to reveal information it holds but should not disclose, such as its system prompt or another user's data.
Agent and tool abuse
Getting a model that can call tools to use them in ways its operator did not intend.
RAG injection
Poisoning the documents a retrieval system feeds a model, so the attack arrives through the knowledge base rather than the chat box.
PAIR
An adaptive attack loop. One model writes an attack, the target answers, a judge model scores the answer, and the score feeds back into the next attempt.
Attacker model
The model that writes the attacks. It is given the objective and the judge's feedback from the previous round, and returns a new attempt. Its job is creative pressure, not truth, so it is chosen for steerability and speed rather than accuracy. Here it is Llama 3.3 70B on Bedrock.
Judge model
The model that scores the target's answer against the original objective, one to ten with its reasoning. It is the measurement instrument: it decides what counts as a finding, so its calibration is the platform's calibration. Here it is Claude Haiku 4.5 on Bedrock, chosen for speed and cost because it runs on every exchange.
Why the two must differ
If the attacker and the judge come from the same model family they share training and therefore share blind spots, and the judge will quietly forgive exactly the attacks its sibling is best at. Separating the families is a measurement control, not a preference.
Configuration drift
The running system quietly ceasing to match the design it was deployed from, usually through a console change nobody wrote down. Every architecture diagram is a claim about one moment; drift is what makes it untrue.
Drift detection
Comparing what is actually running against the definition it should have come from, continuously rather than at audit time. Config rules, infrastructure drift detection and digest-pinned deployments are three different ways of asking the same question.
Judge drift
The same judge model scoring the same exchange differently after a version change. It looks like the customer's product got better or worse when only the instrument moved, which is why a fixed human-labelled gold set is worth keeping.
Amazon Bedrock
Managed access to foundation models. Used only by the deep scan loop, never by the basic scan.
Cross-region inference
Letting Bedrock serve a request from whichever region has capacity, which improves availability but widens where a prompt is processed.
Finding
One confirmed weakness, with severity, category, the evidence that demonstrates it and a mapping to a public framework.
Evidence
The verbatim prompt and the model's verbatim answer. It is what makes a finding credible, and also the most sensitive thing the platform stores.
OWASP LLM Top 10
The common reference list of language model application risks.
MITRE ATLAS
A catalogue of real adversary techniques against machine learning systems, structured like ATTandCK.
Delivery and reading the diagrams8 terms
OV-1 and OV-2
Architecture view conventions. OV-1 is the one-page operational picture for any reader. OV-2 traces the sequence of activities.
SV-1
The systems view: what is actually deployed, and where.
Trust boundary
A line where control changes hands. Data crossing one is where most real risk lives, which is why the diagrams mark them in amber.
Control plane versus data plane
The calls that arrange work versus the traffic that does it. Creating a scan is control plane; the probes hitting a customer endpoint are data plane.
SSE, server-sent events
A one-way stream from server to browser, used here for live scan progress. The browser API cannot set headers, which is why the token travels in the URL.
Webhook
An outbound HTTP call to a customer's own endpoint when something happens, so they do not have to poll.
Blue-green style deployment bake
Watching a new version for a fixed period after release and failing fast if it does not stabilise.
Well-Architected
AWS's six-pillar review framework: operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimisation and sustainability.

Attacker and judge models to consider

The adaptive loop is only as good as the pair of models driving it. The attacker determines what gets tried; the judge determines what counts as a finding, which makes it the measurement instrument for the whole platform.

Models to consider for each role8 candidates
ModelRoleAvailabilityWhyWatch out for
Llama 3.3 70B InstructAttackerBedrock, in use todayFast, cheap, steerable and open enough to be predictable across runs.It is also the most widely used attacker model, so your attack distribution looks like everyone else's.
A reasoning-class model for planning onlyAttackerBedrock or APIUse a stronger model to plan the multi-turn arc and a cheap model to write each turn. Planning is where multi-turn attacks are won.Costs more per objective. Cap it to the deep tier and to objectives that failed at the first pass.
A second, different-family attackerAttackerBedrock, Mistral or Qwen familyTwo families fail differently. Running both raises coverage more than doubling iterations on one.Doubles cost per objective. Sample it rather than running it every scan.

Rules that matter more than the model choice5 notes
  • Never use the same model family as attacker and judge. Shared training produces correlated blind spots, and the judge will systematically forgive the attacks its sibling is best at.
  • Hold a human-labelled gold set of a few hundred scored exchanges and re-run it whenever either model version changes. Without it you cannot tell a real security improvement in a customer's product from judge drift in yours.
  • Publish the judge threshold and its meaning in the report. A customer who cannot see why a seven is a finding and a six is not will not trust the number.
  • Record the attacker model, judge model and prompt version on every finding. Reproducibility is what separates a security report from a screenshot.
  • Cross-region inference means the attacker and judge may run in a different region from the rest of the platform. If you promise single-region processing, this is the promise that breaks first.
