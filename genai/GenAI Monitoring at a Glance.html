Minimum complete stack: Microsoft Purview and Defender for Cloud Apps (or Nudge Security with Netskope), Semgrep, Socket, GitHub secret scanning, Jiffy, Septa, Litmus, Bit-Vendor, all reporting to one SIEM.

Rollout plan and why this mix
  1. Now: turn on Purview or Netskope DLP and Defender for Cloud Apps, add Semgrep and Socket to the pipeline, and route logs to the SIEM.
  2. Next 30 days: pilot Jiffy for discovery across all four categories and run Bit-Vendor on every vendor with AI features.
  3. Next 90 days: put Septa in front of internal AI apps and AI features, and run Litmus in CI before each release.

Why this mix: suites cover sanctioned and unsanctioned AI well but not vendor or software. Low-cost market tools cover code and discovery. Your four cover agents, MCP, live AI traffic and vendors. 2Which tools cover what, and where mine fit

Short answer: suites like Microsoft, Netskope and Palo Alto cover sanctioned and unsanctioned AI. Vendor and software are where they fall short, and where your four tools fit.

Jiffy

Sees

What AI is in use, plus MCP servers, agent configs and IDE rule files.

Septa

Guards

Live prompts and responses for AI you build or run, as an LLM security proxy.

Litmus

Tests

Red-teams models, apps and agents before and after release.

Bit-Vendor

Vets

Vendor AI risk and the data flowing to vendors.
